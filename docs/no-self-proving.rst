随笔九十六 谁主张谁举证（no self-proving）
================================================================================================================================================================================

永不自证
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

不自证像一把剪刀，剪断对方投射的情绪绳索，让对话回到事实本身，而非陷入“举证-反驳-再举证”的无限循环。

不自证 ≠ 不回应，而是：

把举证责任还回去：“你这样说，有证据吗？”

把情绪隔离出去：“你生气，不代表我错。”

把对话拉回事实：“我们只谈发生了什么，不谈动机。”

不自证，是给对方一个镜子，而不是一个靶子。

它让吵架从“你死我活”变成“你吵你的，我稳我的”，最终让情绪落地，问题浮出水面。

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The phrase "no self-proving" in the context of a conflict or quarrel refers to the idea that an individual's claims, perspective, or arguments cannot be taken as automatically valid or true simply because they assert or believe them to be so. It emphasizes that no one party can "prove" their correctness or innocence simply through their own declarations or subjective viewpoint. This concept is rooted in the broader idea that proof or truth often requires objective evidence, outside validation, or mutual understanding, rather than relying solely on one's personal judgment, emotions, or biases.

**Detailed Explanation:**

**The Nature of Conflicts and Quarrels:**

In disagreements, whether personal, professional, or societal, each party typically believes that their perspective is correct, justified, or superior.
However, just because someone thinks or says they are right does not inherently make it so. A person's perspective is subjective—it stems from their own experiences, beliefs, and biases, which may not align with the facts or the other party's perspective.
"Self-Proving" and Its Flaws:

"Self-proving" would mean validating one's position based solely on one's own assertions, without external evidence or agreement. For example, a statement like, "I'm right because I know I am," is self-proving—it relies only on the individual’s inner conviction.
This approach is flawed in a conflict because:

It dismisses the other party's perspective.

It lacks objective evidence or rational argumentation.

It can lead to stalemates where two sides refuse to acknowledge each other’s point of view.

**Why "No Self-Proving" Matters:**

Fairness: In a conflict, fairness requires that neither party gets to assume they are right without offering valid reasons, evidence, or mutual discussion.

Objectivity: Conflicts are best resolved when both parties evaluate the matter based on facts, shared values, or mutually agreed-upon frameworks, rather than insisting on their subjective feelings or assumptions.
When there is "no self-proving," it means the burden is on both sides to substantiate their claims with reasons or evidence, rather than simply asserting superiority.
Examples:

Personal Conflict: Suppose two friends are arguing about who was responsible for forgetting to make dinner reservations. One says, "I know it's your fault because I remember telling you!" The other responds, "It's not my fault because I would never forget something important!" These are self-proving arguments because neither can back up their claims with objective evidence (e.g., text messages, prior agreements, or clear communication). Resolution requires moving beyond self-assumed correctness.

Professional Conflict: In a workplace, an employee might claim, "I deserve the promotion because I work the hardest!" But if that claim isn't supported by measurable outcomes (e.g., performance reviews, productivity metrics), it becomes self-proving. The decision requires evidence beyond personal belief.
Steps to Resolve a Conflict Without Self-Proving:

Provide Evidence: Instead of relying on personal conviction, present tangible evidence to support your claims (e.g., facts, documents, examples).

Listen to the Other Side: Understand the opposing perspective and acknowledge its validity where applicable.

Seek Neutral Mediation: If both parties are entrenched in self-proving arguments, a neutral third party can facilitate resolution by evaluating the situation objectively.

Focus on Problem-Solving: Shift the discussion from "who’s right" to "how can we resolve this?"

**Philosophical Perspective:**

In philosophy, arguments or claims are valid when they are logical, well-reasoned, and supported by evidence. A self-proving argument is often considered a form of circular reasoning (e.g., "I'm right because I'm never wrong"), which is invalid because it doesn't provide external justification.
Epistemologically, humans have cognitive biases that make self-proving arguments inherently flawed—people tend to favor their own perspectives, and without checks (like evidence or external validation), this bias can perpetuate conflict.

**Conclusion:**

In essence, "no self-proving" in a conflict means that neither party can act as their own judge or jury to validate their stance. Resolution requires moving the discussion away from subjective assertions toward objectivity, fairness, evidence, and mutual understanding. It prevents disputes from degenerating into "he said, she said" dynamics, fostering healthier communication and more equitable outcomes.
