随笔211 This Is Water
===================================================

演讲全名：This Is Water: Some Thoughts, Delivered on a Significant Occasion, about Living a Compassionate Life
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------


Greetings, and congratulations to Kenyon’s graduating class of 2005.
  
There are these two young fish swimming along, and they happen to meet an older fish swimming the other way, who nods at them and says, “Morning, boys. How’s the water?”
And the two young fish swim on for a bit, and then eventually one of them looks over at the other and goes, “What the hell is water?”

This is a standard requirement of US commencement speeches, the deployment of didactic little parable-ish stories. The story thing turns out to be one of the better, less bullshitty conventions of the genre. But if you’re worried that I plan to present myself here as the wise older fish explaining what water is to you younger fish, please don’t be. I am not the wise old fish.

The point of the fish story is merely that the most obvious, important realities are often the ones that are hardest to see and talk about. Stated as an English sentence, of course, this is just a banal platitude, but the fact is that in the day-to-day trenches of adult existence, banal platitudes can have a life-or-death weight.

The whole point of the liberal arts education you’ve received is to give you the freedom to choose what to pay attention to, to have awareness of your own default selfish mindset.

key  awareness
-------------------------------

“It means being conscious and aware enough to choose what you pay attention to and to choose how you construct meaning from experience.”

“Our default setting is to be self-centered; education’s purpose is to break that automatic, blind self-focus through constant awareness.”

“Everybody worships. The only choice we get is what to worship. And the compelling reason for maybe choosing some sort of god or spiritual-type thing to worship is that pretty much anything else you worship will eat you alive.”




大卫・福斯特・华莱士在《这就是水》毕业演讲中以小鱼不识水的寓言开篇，指出人们终日被自我中心的本能思维包裹却浑然不觉，这种凡事以自身得失为标尺的 “思维默认模式”，会让我们在琐碎日常里陷入烦躁、狭隘与麻木；他提出博雅教育真正的价值不在于知识与谋生技能，而在于唤醒清醒觉知，让人拥有自主选择注意力与解读世界的自由，在堵车、排队、琐事缠身的平凡困境中主动跳出自私视角，看见他人各有煎熬与难处，主动共情、心怀悲悯，有意识地拒绝无意识的刻薄，以持续的自我觉察践行温柔包容的人生，这才是成年人真正的精神自由与有同理心的活法。

Delivered at Kenyon College, This Is Water uses the fish parable to illustrate that people are blind to the self-centered “default setting” that surrounds them like water; Wallace argues the true value of liberal education is conscious awareness, empowering us to actively choose our perspective amid tedious daily life, transcend instinctive selfishness, recognize everyone bears hidden struggles, and live a mindful, compassionate life instead of being trapped in unconscious bitterness.
